The craft · Field notes

Good masonry is quiet work. It just stands there, for decades.

Brick, block, stone, and concrete are unforgiving materials. Laid right, they need almost nothing from you. Laid wrong, they tell on themselves every rainy season.

A masonry contractor with four decades behind the trowel has seen every way a wall can fail, and every way to keep it from failing. Garetto Chenny has held a California masonry contractor license since March 1983, working out of Burbank through all of it. That kind of tenure is not common in this trade, and it shows up in the details: level courses, tight joints, proper flashing, and footings that respect the soil they sit on.

Most of the calls a mason gets are not for brand-new showpieces. They are for the chimney that started shedding brick, the retaining wall that began to lean, the steps that settled, the stucco-era veneer that needs a careful match. Repair and restoration are where experience pays for itself, because matching old mortar color and texture is part science, part memory.

"Square, level, and plumb. The rest is just patience." Garetto Chenny · Burbank, CA

If you are planning a patio, a garden wall, a mailbox, or a full chimney rebuild, the first step is a conversation and a look at the site. Call (818) 562-3113 and describe what you are seeing. A seasoned mason can usually tell you in minutes whether you are looking at a repair or a rebuild.

Services · The trade

What a mason actually does

Six corners of the masonry trade, in plain language. Call to confirm which services are offered for your project and to talk through an estimate.

01

Brick and Block Work

Walls, planters, mailboxes, fences, and garden structures.

The backbone of the trade. A well-laid brick or block wall is structural, handsome, and nearly maintenance free. Matching existing brick on older Burbank homes takes an eye for size, color, and mortar tone, the kind of matching that only comes with years of handling the material.

Ask about: repairs, rebuilds, and new walls.

02

Stone and Veneer

Natural stone and manufactured stone facings.

Stone veneer can transform a plain facade, a fireplace, or an entry column. The difference between a veneer that looks real and one that looks glued on is layout: varied joints, no repeating patterns, and corners that turn like they were cut from one rock.

Ask about: facades, fireplaces, and accent walls.

03

Chimney and Fireplace Masonry

Repairs, rebuilds, crowns, and repointing.

Chimneys take the worst weather a house sees, and they fail from the top down. Spalling brick, crumbling crowns, and washed-out mortar are all fixable, but a chimney rebuild is cheaper than the water damage a neglected one causes inside the house.

Ask about: chimney inspection and repair.

04

Concrete and Hardscaping

Patios, walkways, steps, and slabs.

Flatwork lives or dies on the base beneath it. Proper grading, compaction, and control joints keep a patio from cracking and a walkway from heaving. It is unglamorous prep work, and it is the whole job.

Ask about: patios, walkways, and steps.

05

Retaining Walls

Segmental, block, and stone walls that hold grade.

A retaining wall is a drainage project wearing a masonry costume. Weep holes, gravel backfill, and proper batter matter more than the face stone. Build the drainage right and the wall stands for generations.

Ask about: walls for slopes and terraces.

06

Tuckpointing and Restoration

Repointing mortar joints on aging brickwork.

Mortar is sacrificial by design: it is supposed to weather so the brick does not have to. Repointing, done with the right mortar mix for the era of the building, buys an old wall another few decades without touching the brick itself.

Ask about: mortar restoration and matching.
