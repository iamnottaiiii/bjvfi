5 questions to ask any contractor

Generic, practical questions that apply to any contractor interview. Bring this list to every estimate appointment.

A clear contractor explains how estimates work, how scheduling is handled, who will be on site, and how the job wraps up. Vague answers here are a yellow flag for any project.
Get the scope, materials, labor, timeline, and payment terms in writing. Ask how changes are priced if the plan shifts mid-project, and make sure both sides sign off on the same document.
Know whether the person you meet is the person on site, whether subcontractors are used, and who your single point of contact is during the job. Good communication prevents most project headaches.
Every project can hit a snag. Ask how change orders are approved, how delays are communicated, and what happens if hidden problems are found behind walls or under floors.
Past projects similar to yours are the best predictor of fit. Ask for references you can contact and, where possible, photos of completed work in the same category as your project.
Tip: Ask these same five questions of every contractor you interview, then compare the answers side by side. Consistent, specific answers beat smooth sales talk every time.

Services checklist

Every project below is shown as a decision checklist: what makes it a good fit, and what to think through first. These are common general-contractor project categories shown to help you compare options.

Please note: Project categories below are Sample examples for comparison. Call (216) 373-7070 to confirm which project types Right Choice Contractors currently takes on.

Kitchen updates Sample

  • Good fit when: you want better layout, storage, or finishes in the busiest room of the house.
  • Good fit when: cabinets, counters, or flooring are worn past simple repair.
  • Consider: kitchen work affects plumbing and electrical, so plan for a realistic timeline.

Bathroom updates Sample

  • Good fit when: fixtures leak, tile fails, or the layout no longer works for your household.
  • Good fit when: you want safer, more accessible features as needs change.
  • Consider: moisture and ventilation details matter more here than in any other room.

Roofing and exteriors Sample

  • Good fit when: you see missing shingles, leaks, or siding damage after storms.
  • Good fit when: the exterior is aging and hurting curb appeal or efficiency.
  • Consider: exterior work is weather dependent, so scheduling needs flexibility.

Basements and living space Sample

  • Good fit when: you need more usable square footage without moving.
  • Good fit when: an unfinished area could become a family room, office, or guest space.
  • Consider: moisture control and egress requirements come first in below-grade spaces.

Additions Sample

  • Good fit when: your household has outgrown the current footprint.
  • Good fit when: you plan to stay long term and want the space done right.
  • Consider: additions involve permits and inspections, so expect a longer planning phase.

General repairs Sample

  • Good fit when: small problems are stacking up and you want one reliable point of contact.
  • Good fit when: drywall, trim, doors, or fixtures need attention across the house.
  • Consider: bundling smaller repairs into one visit is usually more efficient.

How to read contractor reviews like a pro

Look for specifics

Reviews that mention the type of project, the timeline, and how problems were handled tell you far more than star counts alone.

Check the responses

See whether the business replies to reviews, especially critical ones. A thoughtful response says a lot about accountability.

Note the dates

Recent reviews reflect the current crew and current standards. A strong record this year matters more than one from five years ago.
