Why a survey matters

Guessing costs more than measuring.

A professional survey settles the questions that turn neighborly: where the lot line really runs, whether the driveway crosses it, and what you actually own before you buy, build, or fence.

Buying or selling

Lenders and title companies often want the boundaries confirmed before money changes hands. A survey gives every party the same drawing to work from.

Fences and disputes

A few inches of fence in the wrong place can become a long argument. A surveyor sets the line with instruments, not opinions.

Building and adding on

Setbacks, easements, and lot coverage all start from the true boundary. Measure first, pour concrete second.

How it usually goes

From first call to finished plat.

General information

Every surveyor runs the job a little differently, but the shape of the work is standard across Ohio. This is general information about the profession, not a description of this office's process.

The call

Have your street address handy, and your deed or prior survey if you can find it. The surveyor will ask what you need the survey for: a closing, a fence, a build, or a dispute.

Records research

Before anyone visits, the surveyor pulls deeds, prior plats, and county records to reconstruct the legal history of your parcel and its neighbors.

Field work

A crew visits with a total station and GPS, hunting for original monuments, measuring angles and distances, and setting new markers where corners are lost.

The plat

You receive a signed, sealed drawing showing boundaries, dimensions, monuments, and easements: the document closings, permits, and courts rely on.
