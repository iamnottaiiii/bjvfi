Why insulation matters

Most of a home's heat moves through the ceiling.

In Valley heat, an under-insulated attic turns the ceiling into a radiator. The right insulation slows that transfer, evens out room temperatures and lets the air conditioner cycle instead of running all afternoon.

Sample artwork
  • Attics in older Valley homes often carry thin, settled insulation that no longer performs.
  • Air leaks around can lights, vents and top plates let conditioned air escape before insulation can do its job.
  • A proper job pairs air sealing with the right insulation depth for the space.
  • Call for an assessment and a clear written scope before any work begins.
What to expect

A straightforward insulation visit

Every home is different, but a typical insulation project follows the same honest sequence.

  • Look: the attic is inspected for insulation depth, air leaks and access.
  • Seal: gaps and bypasses are closed so new insulation performs.
  • Fill: the chosen insulation is installed to the agreed depth.
  • Verify: the finished attic is reviewed with you before sign-off.

Services

The insulation stack

Typical scopes of an insulation contractor. Call to confirm what your home needs.

1

Attic Insulation

The most common job: bringing attic insulation up to an effective depth so the ceiling stops leaking heat in both directions.

2

Blown-In Insulation

Loose-fill insulation blown across the attic floor, filling around joists, wiring and odd corners that batts miss.

3

Batt Insulation

Pre-cut fiberglass batts fitted between studs and joists, a clean option for open, accessible cavities.

4

Air Sealing

Closing gaps around fixtures, chases and top plates before insulation goes in, so conditioned air stays inside.

5

Insulation Removal

Taking out old, compressed or contaminated insulation so the new layer starts clean.

6

Radiant Barrier

Reflective foil under the roof deck that bounces radiant heat back out, a strong pairing with attic insulation in Valley sun.
