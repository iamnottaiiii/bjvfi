Why irrigation matters

A green lawn is a system, not luck

Georgia summers punish weak sprinkler systems. Brown patches, soggy low spots, and climbing water bills usually trace back to the same culprits: broken heads, tired valves, bad pressure, or a controller nobody has touched in years.

Repairs that hold

Broken heads, leaking valves, and wiring faults diagnosed properly the first time, so the same zone does not fail again next month.

Water where it belongs

Head alignment and pressure balancing put water on the grass, not the driveway, which keeps the landscape even and the bill in check.

Controllers set right

Timers programmed for the season and the soil, with rain shutoff where the system supports it, so watering runs on schedule without waste.

One contractor, whole system

From the backflow device to the last rotor head, every part of the system is one company's responsibility.

What to expect

A service call, step by step

  • Walk the zones. Every zone runs while the technician watches each head and listens for valve trouble.
  • Plain-language findings. You hear what is wrong, what it costs, and what can wait, before any work begins.
  • Repair and retest. Heads, valves, and wiring are fixed, then every zone runs again to confirm even coverage.

Common fixes

Problems we solve every week

If any of these sound familiar, the system is telling you it needs attention.

01
Dry spots and brown patches

Usually clogged nozzles, sunken heads, or a zone that never fully pressurizes.

02
Water on pavement

Misaligned or broken heads throwing water onto driveways and sidewalks instead of turf.

03
A zone that will not shut off

A classic stuck valve. It floods one area and starves the rest until it is replaced.

04
Controller confusion

Dead zones after a power outage or a timer nobody has reprogrammed in years.
