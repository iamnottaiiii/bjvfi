01 · how we work

Start with the wood,
not the catalog

Every piece starts as rough lumber and a conversation. Bring measurements, a photo of the spot it is going in, or just an idea. We will talk through the wood, the finish, and the budget, then build it to fit your home, not someone else's warehouse.

  • Custom pieces sized to your space, down to the inch
  • Solid lumber and traditional joinery, not particle board
  • Repairs and refinishing that keep good furniture out of the landfill

Measure twice, cut once. There is no shortcut around that, and we would not take one if there were.

02 · the craft

Built to be kept,
not replaced

A well-built piece of furniture outlives its maker. The shop builds with that in mind: solid lumber, joints that hold, and finishes you can refresh instead of replace.

It also means the shop can usually bring a tired piece back to life. A wobbly chair, a scratched tabletop, a dresser with stuck drawers, repair is part of the craft, and it almost always costs less than buying new.

The shop story

A working shop
on Decoursey Ave

Milt Mc Cracken's Wood Shop is a Covington woodworking shop, the kind with sawdust on the floor and a bench that has seen some things.

the shop

Hand tools, sharp blades,
no shortcuts

There is a particular smell to a real wood shop, cut pine, linseed oil, coffee. At 4111 Decoursey Ave, that smell means someone is building something meant to last.

The shop works the way woodworkers have worked for generations: measure carefully, cut once, fit every joint by hand. It is slower than a factory, and that is the entire point. A table that took its time will still be standing when the flat-pack one is long gone.

Customers come for custom furniture and home decor, the shop's bread and butter, and stay for repairs and refinishing, because a shop that can build can usually save what someone else built.

Wood remembers how it was treated. So we treat it well.

why custom

Your home is not
a showroom floor

Rooms have quirks: the wall that is not quite straight, the nook that is exactly 31 inches wide, the doorway the couch barely cleared. Custom woodwork turns those quirks into features instead of compromises.

It also means you choose the wood. Oak for strength, pine for warmth and price, walnut when you want to make a statement. The shop will walk you through the trade-offs in plain language, because an informed customer is a happy one.
