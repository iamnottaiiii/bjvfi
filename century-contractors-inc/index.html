Material study

Anatomy of a mudded joint

A cross section of what hides behind a flat wall: two panels, one seam, tape, and compound built up in coats.

  • 1Tape coat. Joint tape is bedded into wet compound over the seam, pressed flat, bubbles worked out.
  • 2Fill coats. Each coat goes on wider than the last, filling the joint recess and feathering the edges.
  • 3Finish coat. A thin final pass, sanded smooth, leaves no ridge for paint to reveal.

Finish levels, the industry standard

Drywall finishing is graded on a five level scale used across the trade. Higher levels mean more coats of compound and a smoother wall under paint. Use it to describe what you want, then call (440) 232-2626 to confirm scope and pricing.

1

Fire taping

Tape set in joint compound over every joint and fastener. Tool marks and ridges are acceptable.

Best for: areas hidden from view, like above ceilings or inside service shafts.

1 coat over tape
2

Utility finish

Level 1 plus one thin coat of compound over the tape, joints, and fasteners.

Best for: garages, storage rooms, and other utility spaces where appearance is secondary.

2 coats
3

Texture ready

Level 2 plus an additional coat. The surface is prepared for heavy texture or heavyweight wallcovering.

Best for: walls that will carry a pronounced texture hiding minor tool marks.

3 coats
4

Paint ready

Three coats of compound over tape, joints, and fasteners, sanded smooth. The common standard for living spaces.

Best for: flat and eggshell paints in normal lighting, the everyday residential finish.

3 full coats
5

Skim coated

Level 4 plus a thin skim coat of compound over the entire surface, equalizing paper and compound absorption.

Best for: glossy paints and walls in harsh raking light where every flaw would show.

Full skim coat

Not sure which?

Describe the room, the paint you plan to use, and the light it gets. A short conversation usually settles the level in minutes.
