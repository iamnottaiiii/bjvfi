01 The Cover Story

Where Cincinnati Brings Its Bent Fenders

For decades, one small shop on West Seymour Avenue has straightened the dents of the north side. This is the feature on Stoinoff’s Valley Auto Body, told like it deserves to be told.

By The Shop Journal StaffCincinnati, Ohio

Some shops advertise. This one never had to. Stoinoff’s Valley Auto Body sits at 1088 West Seymour Avenue in Cincinnati, and for the better part of fifty years it has been the place neighbors send neighbors when a fender meets a pole, a door meets a shopping cart, or a Michigan winter’s worth of road salt finally wins.

Reviewers keep returning to the same words: local, honest, fair. One longtime customer wrote that they have brought many cars here over the years and called the work great, adding that the owner works well with insurance companies. Another called it a local owned business that has been there for decades, one that will work with you and even offer new and recycled parts to fit the budget.

“I have taken many cars here over the years. Great work.”From a customer review, 2017

That is the whole editorial in one line. A shop does not survive on Seymour Avenue for that long on luck. It survives on straight panels, fair prices, and people who come back with the next car.

03 The Profile

Fifty Years on Seymour Avenue

What public records and longtime customers tell us about one of Cincinnati’s older neighborhood body shops.

A shop history, assembled from the recordPage 03

Every city has shops like this one: the kind you find because somebody’s uncle swears by it. Stoinoff’s Valley Auto Body has occupied 1088 West Seymour Avenue long enough that its name is stitched into the north side’s word of mouth. Public business records trace the shop back to 1976, and directories credit it with decades of continuous operation as a small, locally owned body shop.

What the record shows is a working shop, not a showroom. Reviewers describe a practical place: fair with pricing, willing to work with insurance companies, and open about using new or recycled parts depending on the budget. It is the kind of shop where the estimate comes with a conversation.

“Local owned business, been there for decades, and they will work with you.”From a customer review, 2021

Public reviews also tell a fuller story. One review thread recounts a payment dispute from years ago, with the shop’s side of the story defended at length by a representative. Like many small shops with long histories, the record here is mixed, and we print it as we found it on the Reviews page.

The through line, across the positive reviews, is loyalty: customers who brought one car, then another, then another. In an industry built on one bad day per customer, repeat business is the only review that really counts.
