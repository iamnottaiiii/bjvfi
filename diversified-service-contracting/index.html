Why homeowners hire a diversified contractor

Most projects touch more than one trade. Coordinating them yourself costs time and invites finger-pointing. Here is the case for one accountable builder.

A kitchen remodel is never just a kitchen remodel. It is plumbing rough-in, electrical, drywall, flooring, and finish carpentry, each depending on the one before it. When one contractor owns the whole sequence, the schedule holds and the details line up.

That is the idea behind the name. Diversified Service Contracting brings the trades together under one roof in New Cumberland, so you make one call, sign one agreement, and hold one team accountable for the result.

One call, one agreement, one team accountable for the result.The diversified approach

It starts with a walkthrough at your property. We listen to what you want, flag what the structure will and will not allow, and put together a written scope you can actually read. No allowances buried in fine print, no allowances at all unless you ask for them.

About

A contractor shaped like your project list.

Diversified Service Contracting works out of New Cumberland, Pennsylvania, serving homeowners across Cumberland County and the West Shore. The business was built around a simple observation: most home projects do not fit neatly into one trade, so the contractor should not either.

As a general contractor, we plan the whole job, sequence the trades, and stand behind the finished work. Plumbing, electrical, carpentry, drywall, tile, paint: whatever your project calls for, it arrives in the right order with one team managing it.

We plan the whole job, sequence the trades, and stand behind the finished work.How we work

Our process is deliberately old-fashioned. We visit in person, we write down exactly what is included, and we keep the jobsite clean enough that you would not know we were there. Payment follows progress you can see and inspect, never the other way around.

If you are comparing contractors, ask each one the same three questions: who is my single point of contact, what exactly is in writing before work starts, and what happens if something is not right at the end. We are comfortable with all three answers.
