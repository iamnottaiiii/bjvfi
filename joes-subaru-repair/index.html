03 // Specialist Q and A

Subaru Questions, Answered

Common Subaru owner questions. These answers are general information, not verified shop policy. For your specific car, call the shop.

Sample answer
Certain older Subaru boxer engines are known for external head gasket leaks, often spotted as oil or coolant seepage. A specialist checks this pattern first instead of chasing symptoms. Sample
Sample answer
Subaru's symmetrical all wheel drive is sensitive to differences in tire circumference. Mismatched tires can stress the center differential, so matched sets are the safe move. Sample
Sample answer
On belt driven Subaru engines, replacement is commonly recommended around the 100,000 mile mark, but the exact interval depends on your model and year. Check your owner's manual or call the shop. Sample
Sample answer
Some Subaru engines consume oil as they age, especially past 100,000 miles. Regular level checks between changes prevent low oil damage. If consumption is heavy, have it diagnosed. Sample
Sample answer
Recall work must go through a franchised dealer. For everything else, an independent specialist can often do the same job for less. Call to confirm what the shop takes on. Sample
