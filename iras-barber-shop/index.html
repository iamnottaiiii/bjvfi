Straight Razor Shaves, Tight Cuts, and Not a Salon in Sight

One reviewer summed up the shop's creed: if you want a saloon and a massage, this is not your shop. If you want a good haircut at a good price, walk in.

Walk-Ins Welcome 4.9 Stars, 168 Reviews Est. 1963

On Monmouth Street in downtown Newport stands a barber shop of the old school. Ira's is the kind of place reviewers describe as a refreshing return to a simpler time: classic cuts, hot towel shaves finished with a straight razor, and barbers who talk with you, not past you. Conversation runs from sports to politics to science fiction, and University of Kentucky memorabilia lines the walls.

The shop keeps hours for working people: open at 7 in the morning, Tuesday through Saturday, with free public parking right next door. Reviewers report that walk-ins are the norm here, appointments are not the custom, and if there is a wait it is a short one. Kids are welcome, and more than one parent has written about bringing in a nervous teenager and watching him leave happy.

Price comes up in nearly every review, and always with surprise at how low it runs. Customers online report paying about ten dollars for a haircut, with the hot towel shave called complimentary more than once. Prices change over time, so the shop itself is the authority: call ahead and ask. What does not change, by all public accounts, is the standard of the cut.

The verdict across public listings is near unanimous: 4.9 stars from 168 reviews. Readers will find the full public record, including the few less glowing notices, on our Reviews page.

The Ledger. Sixty three years and counting.

A Newport Institution Since 1963

Public directories date Ira's Barber Shop to 1963 and credit it with more than six decades in business, all from a single location on Monmouth Street.

Every town has a barber shop that outlasts the trends, and in Newport that shop is Ira's. Public business directories list its founding in 1963, making it one of the longest running barber shops in Northern Kentucky. It has done it from one address, 707 Monmouth St, with a small staff and a simple formula: honest cuts at honest prices.

The owner is listed in public directories as Ira Pyles. Reviewers over the years mention barbers Ira and Chris by name, describing a family run feel and conversations that keep customers coming back as much as the cuts do. Sports talk runs strong here, and the walls carry University of Kentucky memorabilia, a detail more than one visitor has noted with approval.

What the shop is not matters as much as what it is. It is not a salon, it is not a spa, and reviewers are quick to say so with affection. It is a place where a man has had his hair cut the same way since boyhood, where the price barely moves, and where a newcomer from New York or California can walk in a stranger and walk out a regular.
