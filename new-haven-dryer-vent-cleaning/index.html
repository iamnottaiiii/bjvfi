The short version

The 60 minute job that protects the whole house

The U.S. Fire Administration estimates about 2,900 home clothes dryer fires each year, and failure to clean is the leading contributing factor. Your lint screen only catches part of it. The rest packs into the vent run where you cannot see it.

Most dryer vent clogs build the same way: a little lint escapes the screen every load, coats the inside of the duct, and narrows the airway month after month. The dryer runs hotter and longer. Clothes come out damp. The laundry room feels humid. Those are not signs of an old dryer. They are signs of a blocked vent.

If drying takes two cycles, the vent is doing less work than the lint is.

A professional cleaning reaches the full run with rotary brushes and high airflow tools, including the elbows and the exterior hood where lint and even bird nests collect. The result is simple to measure: strong airflow at the outside vent and a dryer that finishes on time.

Why vent cleaning matters

5 signs your vent is clogged

  1. Clothes take two cycles or come out damp
  2. The dryer feels hot to the touch
  3. A burning smell in the laundry room
  4. The laundry room gets unusually humid
  5. The outside vent flap barely opens

The real blockage sits in the duct: elbows packed with lint, transition hose pinched behind the machine, and the exterior hood caked shut. Our standard visit addresses the full path because a partial cleaning leaves the worst of it behind.

Why it matters

Lint is quiet. That is what makes it dangerous.

New haven Dryer Vent Cleaning is a New Haven service focused on one job: keeping dryer vents clear, safe, and working the way they were designed to.

Dryer fires do not start with drama. They start with a vent that moves a little less air each month, a dryer that runs a little hotter to compensate, and lint packed tight against warm metal. The U.S. Fire Administration points to failure to clean as the leading factor in home dryer fires, ahead of mechanical and electrical failures.

The lint screen is the beginning of maintenance, not the whole job.

That is why we built this service around the vent run itself. The screen, the transition hose, the elbows, the long duct through the wall, and the exterior hood each get attention. When the full path is clear, three things happen at once: laundry dries on time, the dryer stops overheating, and a major fire risk drops.
