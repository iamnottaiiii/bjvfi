Roof Replacement

When repairs stop making sense, a full replacement resets the clock. Old roofing comes off, the deck gets inspected and repaired, and new underlayment, flashing, and roofing go on to current standards.

  • Shingle, tile, and flat roofing systems
  • Deck repair and new underlayment
  • Flashing, vents, and skylight curbs redone
  • Cleanup and final walkthrough
Get a replacement estimate

How long does it take?

Most residential replacements run a few days to a week depending on size, pitch, and material. You get a timeline in writing before work starts, and a crew that keeps to it.

Contact us

Roof Repair

A good repair starts with diagnosis. Water rarely enters where it shows up inside, so the work is half detective work: tracing stains back to failed flashing, lifted shingles, or cracked boots around vents and pipes.

  • Leak tracing and source repair
  • Shingle and tile replacement
  • Flashing, valleys, and vent seals
  • Honest advice when repair is not enough
Report a leak

Repair or replace?

If the roof is near the end of its service life, patching buys months at the price of years. A straight answer on which one you are looking at is part of every estimate.

Contact us

Roof Inspection

An inspection is the cheapest roofing service there is, and the one that saves the most money. It documents the roof's condition so small problems get scheduled instead of becoming emergencies.

  • Full surface, flashing, and drainage check
  • Attic and interior leak signs reviewed
  • Written findings with prioritized fixes
  • Pre-purchase and pre-storm inspections
Schedule an inspection

When to inspect

After major wind or rain, before buying or selling, and any time you spot granules in the gutters or a new stain on a ceiling. Once a year is a good habit.

Contact us

Storm and Leak Help

Santa Ana winds and winter rains are hard on Valley roofs. When a storm opens up a roof, the first job is stopping the water: emergency tarping and temporary sealing, then a permanent fix once things dry out.

  • Emergency tarping and water stoppage
  • Wind-lifted shingle and tile repair
  • Damage documentation for insurance
  • Permanent repair after the storm passes
Call about storm damage

First, stop the water.

A bucket under the drip is triage, not a fix. If water is coming in, call now: tarping a roof the same day can be the difference between a repair and a remodel.
