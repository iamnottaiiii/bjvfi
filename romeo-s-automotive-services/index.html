The Denville driver’s notebook

Straight talk about keeping your car healthy.

Oil is cheap, engines are not

Regular oil changes are the single best thing you can do for an engine. Fresh oil keeps moving parts lubricated and carries heat and grit away from critical surfaces.

Brakes talk before they fail

Squealing, grinding, or a pedal that feels soft are all early warnings. Brake pads are inexpensive; rotors, calipers, and the car in front of you are not.

Warning lights are a to-do list

A check-engine light can mean anything from a loose gas cap to a failing sensor. A quick diagnostic scan tells you which, before a small fault cascades.

Tires are your contact patch

Rotation and alignment keep wear even, which means better grip, better mileage, and tires that last their full life.
