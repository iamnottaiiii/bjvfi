Why sweep

Creosote does not take a day off

Every fire leaves soot and creosote behind. A regular sweep keeps the flue clear, the draft strong and the risk of a chimney fire low.

What a proper sweep covers

A thorough chimney service is more than a quick brush. It is an inspection, a cleaning and a safety check, top to bottom.

  • Flue brushed clear of soot and creosote buildup
  • Firebox, damper and smoke shelf cleaned and checked
  • Cap, crown and flashing looked over for damage
  • Your room left clean, soot contained while we work

Good to know

Chimney care, the short version

When to call a sweep

  • It has been a year or more since the last sweep.
  • Fires are smoky, smelly or hard to start.
  • You see soot flakes or hear animals in the flue.
  • You just moved in and the chimney history is unknown.

Burn season is sweep season

In Los Angeles, fireplaces sit unused through the warm months, and that is when nests, debris and moisture settle in. A sweep before the first cold snap means the first fire of the season is a safe one.

Chimney pros generally recommend a sweep at least once a year for wood-burning fireplaces, and an inspection any time something looks or smells off.
