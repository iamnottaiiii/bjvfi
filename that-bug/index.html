One sighting is a warning. Ten is an infestation.

Roaches and mice rarely travel alone. The one you saw in the kitchen usually means family nearby, inside walls, under appliances, or behind baseboards.

That is why every That Bug visit starts with an inspection, not a spray can. Find the harborage, treat it properly, and the sightings stop.

Common calls

The pests Queens keeps calling about

Brownstones, garden apartments, corner stores: the borough has its regulars. So do we.

01Cockroaches

German roaches in kitchens and bathrooms. We bait and treat the cracks they breed in, then verify the activity drops.

02Bed bugs

Bites, shed skins, rusty spots on the mattress. Careful room-by-room inspection, then targeted treatment of every hiding place.

03Mice & rats

Scratching in walls, gnawed packaging, droppings. Trapping plus an entry-point survey so they cannot just move back in.

04Ants

Trails to the pantry or the pet bowl. We trace them to the colony instead of chasing individual ants.

05Flies & gnats

Drain flies, fruit flies, and the mystery swarm by the window. We find the breeding source, because that is the only fix.

06Spiders & silverfish

Basements, closets, and bathrooms. Treatment plus moisture and clutter advice to make the space unwelcoming.

Services

Every service, explained plainly

Open a section to see what the visit covers. Inspection always comes first, and you approve the plan before treatment starts.

For houses, apartments, and multi-family buildings anywhere in Queens.

  • Full inspection for roaches, ants, spiders, silverfish, and pantry pests
  • Treatment focused on harborages: kitchens, bathrooms, basements, and utility areas
  • Written notes on entry points and conditions attracting pests
  • Prevention plan so the treatment lasts

Bites and spots on the sheets are only the start. We check the whole sleeping area methodically.

  • Mattress, box spring, headboard, and nightstand inspection
  • Adjacent rooms checked, since bed bugs travel
  • Targeted treatment of cracks, seams, and harborage points
  • Prep checklist and follow-up plan included

Mice and rats in walls, ceilings, kitchens, and storage areas.

  • Trapping strategy matched to the level of activity
  • Entry-point survey around pipes, doors, vents, and foundations
  • Exclusion recommendations to block re-entry
  • Monitoring to confirm the population is gone

German and American roaches, the two species Queens sees most.

  • Harborages mapped: behind stoves and fridges, under sinks, in wall voids
  • Baiting and targeted treatment rather than blanket spraying
  • Sanitation coaching, because food and water drive roach problems
  • Follow-up to confirm activity has stopped

Trailing ants in kitchens, bathrooms, and around foundations.

  • Trails traced back toward the colony or entry point
  • Baiting that workers carry home, instead of repellents that scatter them
  • Exterior inspection for nests near the foundation

Restaurants, delis, offices, and retail spaces where standards are strict and reputations are fragile.

  • Scheduling around your operating hours
  • Service notes that support health inspection readiness
  • Focus on kitchens, dry storage, and waste handling areas

Nests under eaves, in sheds, trees, and wall voids.

  • Nest location and species assessed before approach
  • Removal timed and handled to protect people and pets
  • Tips to discourage rebuilding in the same spot
