Fire, flour, and patience Sample

A tanoor is not just an oven. It is a beehive of clay, heated until the inner walls glow, where flatbreads are slapped directly onto the hot surface and puff up in seconds. It is one of the oldest ways humans have ever baked bread, and it is still the best.

Tanoor Bakery brings that tradition to Hickory Hills. The day starts with dough and ends with trays of baklava, and in between there is the steady rhythm of the oven: pita puffing, manoushe blistering, fatayir turning golden.

The name says it plainly. مخبز وحلويات تنور means bakery and sweets of the tanoor. Bread from the fire, sweets from the case, and a kitchen serving the full Lebanese table.

How a tanoor bakes

  1. The walls heat. A fire burns inside the clay dome until the walls hold steady, even heat.
  2. The dough slaps on. Flattened dough sticks straight to the hot clay, no pan needed.
  3. Seconds, not minutes. Pita puffs into a balloon; manoushe blisters at the edges.
  4. Out it comes. Peeled off the wall, brushed with olive oil, and served warm.

That is why the pita tastes the way it does: no pan, no tray, just dough and fire.
